Exploring the Intrinsic Geometry of Diffusion Models with Constrained Inverse Kinematics

Constrained inverse kinematics gives a diffusion model a data manifold whose geometry is known exactly. We train one conditional model per robot on seven constraint families at once. Its score function recovers the intrinsic dimension of every solution manifold, and straight lines in its latent space decode to motions that stay close to the manifold.

The score counts the free directions

The singular spectrum of the learned score recovers the analytical dimension on all fourteen robot and constraint conditions with Score-SVD\(^\dagger\), including the discrete UR5 \(\mathrm{SE}(3)\) case.

Abstract

Recent studies suggest that diffusion models can recover geometric structure in the data manifolds they are trained on, yet the supporting evidence has so far come mostly from natural-image data, where the underlying geometry itself is unknown. We study this question in a setting where the geometry is analytically tractable: constrained inverse kinematics (IK). Each task-space constraint defines a configuration-space manifold with known intrinsic dimension, giving direct ground truth for evaluating the geometry learned by the model. For each of the 6-DoF UR5 and 7-DoF Franka, we train a single conditional diffusion model across seven constraint families, spanning solution manifolds from discrete IK branches to self-motion manifolds. Our empirical results reveal that the intrinsic dimension recovered from the model's score function matches the analytical degrees of freedom of the corresponding constraint manifold across both robots. Moreover, linear interpolation in the latent space leads to generated solutions that remain close to the appropriate constraint manifold, indicating that the learned representation further captures geometric structure of the constraint family beyond intrinsic dimension alone. Constrained IK therefore offers a controlled setting for studying the intrinsic geometry learned by diffusion models.

A family of constraint manifolds

A constraint fixes \(k\) of the six task-space coordinates of the end effector. The joint configurations that satisfy it form a manifold of intrinsic dimension \(n - k\), where \(n\) is 6 for the UR5 and 7 for the Franka. Under a full \(\mathrm{SE}(3)\) pose, the UR5 keeps only a finite set of isolated IK branches, while the redundant Franka keeps a one-dimensional self-motion manifold.

Walk the latent space

We invert three IK solutions of the same target to the noise level \(\bar t\) with DDIM and span the plane of their latent codes \(x_a\), \(x_b\) and \(x_c\). Every point of the pad is a latent code, and the arm shows the configuration that the model decodes from it. The shading gives the constraint error of that configuration, so the region spanned by the three codes decodes close to the manifold at the paper's noise level and breaks apart at low noise.

Count the knobs

At a configuration on the manifold, we perturb the sample \(K = 50\) times at noise level \(t\), evaluate the learned score at each perturbation, and stack the results as the columns of a matrix \(S\). Directions normal to the manifold carry large singular values because the score pulls samples back onto it, while the \(n - k\) tangent directions stay near zero. Counting the small singular values estimates the intrinsic dimension. The model reads each joint angle through its sine and cosine, so \(S\) has \(2n\) rows.

Results at a glance

The score recovers the intrinsic dimension

Neighbourhood estimators run on the generated configurations fall short of the analytical dimension as it grows. The score-based estimates match it on every positive-dimensional condition, and Score-SVD\(^\dagger\) also returns zero for the isolated UR5 \(\mathrm{SE}(3)\) branches.
